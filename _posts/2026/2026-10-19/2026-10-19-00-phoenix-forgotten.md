<span class="reviewsection">Premise:</span> So ten years ago they released this movie, where we are told about how twenty years ago (that's 30 years ago from now, i hope you're keeping the dates straight) a few kids disappeared after they started investigating the [Phoenix Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Lights). You might guess how things turned out.<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">Under 90 minutes?</span> Yup, a tiny bit under the limit<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Do they say the title?</span> Not really no

<span class="reviewsection">First thing I thought of:</span>

![phoenix-forgotten](phoenix-forgotten.gif)<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Okay, more:</span> Yeah, this was surprisingly fun! The kids are charmingly 90s, the whole Phoenix Light mystery is very cool and the plot escalates in a direction I liked a lot. Very cool. And I just checked, I knew the actress that played Ashley reminded me of something: she was on [***Ultrasound***]({% post_url 2026/2026-01-19/2026-01-19-00-ultrasound %}), a movie want to rewatch one day.<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">A simple way to improve it:</span> I personally would have dropped the love triangle thing they're (not that subtly) hinting at, that felt a bit "well we might as well put this" compared to how tight the rest was.

<span class="reviewsection">Trivia about the IMDb trivia:</span>
> The footage claiming to be from 1997 was shot on modern digital cameras in HD. To simulate the look of lo-fi analog footage, after the special effects were added in, the footage was copied to actual VHS tapes which were then re-digitized to finish editing. The analog defects are therefore real and not simulated. This had the added benefit of making the computer effects look more integrated with the original footage.

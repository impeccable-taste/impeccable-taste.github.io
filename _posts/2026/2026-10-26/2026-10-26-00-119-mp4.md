<span class="reviewsection">Premise:</span> Florence's won a night at this hotel and she decides the best course of action is to use it to kind of go freeform and brainstorm her next standup set. And film it. Oh also she's off her meds. Oh, *also* apparently this is a haunted room in a hotel. Need I say more?<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">Under 90 minutes?</span> yeah, and still...<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Do they say the title?</span> nope

<span class="reviewsection">First thing I thought of:</span>

![119-mp4](119-mp4.gif)<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Okay, more:</span> Sorry, I'm all for both the quirky found footage movies with weird premises, I also am a huge fan of self-imposed limitations because of budget (for example in this case the camera never, ever moves) but you also need to know what to do with the format, specially if you clearly want to have a message. It's a pity because, and I know this could be Impeccable Hyphen Taste's motto, this would have been a really creepy short, if only you had edited the footage a bit more. I mean you're already breaking your premise when you have several files with cuts, so why not just remove the "nothing is happening" bits that are not actually building tension or dread? It sucks to be negative about a, clearly, zero budget movie (one of our kryptonites here), but...<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">A simple way to improve it:</span> From basically the beginning of the film you can see shadows moving right under the camera, and like, for the first fifteen/twenty minutes I was sure, COMPLETELY SURE that this was a product of, well, zero budget. Your camera/director is accidentally getting in front of the ambient light and keeps forgetting, they don't want to stop filming because, well, every minute counts. Nope, and I found this charmingly hilarious: it is on purpose, around that moment Florence casually brings up shadow people so, uh, I guess those were intended. So, more of it, please.

<span class="reviewsection">Trivia about the IMDb trivia:</span>

Huh, this is a way, way better summary of my issues with the movie. Sometimes brevity *is* the soul of wit...

<span class="reviewsection">Premise:</span> Mārama is this maori woman who travels to England after receiving a letter that promises her some answers about her ancestry. This, being a horror movie, happens but in the most monkey-paw-twitches kind of way, of course.<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">Under 90 minutes?</span> Yeah! A minute less than that!<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Do they say the title?</span> They do! It's her name, after all, although for some time she's called Mary instead.

<span class="reviewsection">First thing I thought of:</span>

![marama](marama.gif)<br/>
<span class="reviewsection">Okay, more:</span> Okay, so, first things first and so nobody says we're not honest here at the Impeccable Taste HQ: yes, at times this movie is way, way into that whole "style over [***substance***]({% post_url 2024/2024-10-18/2024-10-18-00-the-substance %})" thing. Like, all the visuals are gorgeous, but at the same time the movie sometimes goes a bit too hard on the dreamlike/nightmarish bits. Which makes you go "huh?" and "wait, uh?" because, let's face it, i'm not a very intelligent person. On the other hand, this is a really gorgeous folk horror movie that has one of the most uncomfortable, horrific scenes that could have been a hee-hee-haha moment in ***Mad Men*** (blackface included). Very, very uncomfortable movie, impressive photography, some absolutely great casting. And yeah, at times a bit more concerned about how it looks, but hey. There's definitely a LOT being said about cultural appropriation here and it really, really gets horrifying with the metaphors...<br/>

<span class="reviewsection">A simple way to improve it:</span> I loved all the dresses in the movie and also I loved how sweaty and extremely offputting that dude was from literally the first time you see him. Good job there with the casting, really.
